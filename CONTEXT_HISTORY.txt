EORZEA HUNTER: ECHOES OF AN ADVENTURER - CONTEXT HISTORY
=========================================================
This file logs the full context of how this project was requested and built: the user's
messages (verbatim), the provided assets, every major decision, the work done in order,
the tests run and their results, and the known limitations at the time of writing.

---------------------------------------------------------
1. USER REQUEST (verbatim)
---------------------------------------------------------
Attachments: HairstyleSamples.zip, HyurBases.zip, MiqoteBases.zip

"Create a 3D game using VRoid characters. The game should be a .exe to allow for immediate
play upon completion. The game will be a hybrid between Final Fantasy XIV and Monster Hunter,
with some Elden Ring features included. Include in the repository a .txt file that has the
entire context history logged inside. Design this game for maximum performance while keeping
visuals and gameplay high quality. This means whenever a feature is changed then any unused
scripts should be purged, when a feature is depreciated it should not leave behind unused
scripts, and when a feature is added it should be designed to avoid heavy GPU or CPU use when
possible. Lighting and materials should be realistic. Create the game with multiple save slots
rather than simply assuming a single save. Provided is a .zip with the male and female Hyur and
the male and female Miqo'te along with two hairstyle samples and clothing samples for each
race/gender. The hairstyle samples are both on the female Hyur body, but simply remove that
leaving only the hair, and attach it to whatever model is using it. Similarly the clothing
samples have bodies, remove the bodies and isolate each clothing item for it's use.

The gameplay starts with the player creating their adventurer with the same options as in
FFXIV, this includes races, subraces, hairstyles, colors, everything. Add an option to this menu
as well that allows the player to customize a second character, that being what the legendary
Warrior of Light NPC will look like in game. Once the player creates their character(s) they
may choose their starting job. Unlike FFXIV all jobs will be available to choose from at the
start, and each job will play more like a weapon type in Monster Hunter or Elden Ring, as will
player movement and controls. (include gamepad support for menus and gameplay) Once the player
has finished with their character creation, the story begins with their adventurer outside
whichever city is related to their job of choice.

The story is set in Eorzea at the time of post Endwalker, after the Warrior of Light and the
Scions of the Seventh Dawn have thwarted the final days and disbanded. (NOTE: This is a fan
game, so it's important that the game stays true to the lore of FFXIV) The player's character
is a mere adventurer who in the opening cutscene awakes to a concerned villager who found them
naked, battered, and unconscious on the road to the city, likely robbed by bandits. The villager
helps bring the player into the city where they are provided with something simple to wear and
pointed to the local adventurers' guild to find help. The story will continue as the player
plays, keeping them as a mere adventurer. Ensure the cities and regions are accurate recreations
of the ones found in FFXIV. After the player's character has recovered from their wounds, the
guild offers them work in the form of guildleves. The player may then explore the city, where
they can talk to NPCs who have quests to offer. Quests and Guildleves will be similar, but will
differ in rewards and quests will have story elements while Guildleves will just be activities
without any story significance. Quests and Guildleves can be large monster hunts, small monster
hunts, defense missions, resource gathering missions, and escort quests. Alternatively the
player can choose to simply travel through the regions, which are all connected the same way
they are in FFXIV and this will allow the player to find the other cities and regions.

Before the player departs on a quest or guildleve, it is recommended they eat a meal which is
comprised of three slots: The main dish, side, and drink. Each slot has it's own aspects of
gameplay it will target, and each item they choose to fill those slots with will provide their
own buffs or perks for the mission.

Gameplay will be very much like Monster Hunter and Elden Ring, where the player will have a
varied moveset unique to the weapon type they use, and enemies will have telegraphed but heavy
hitting movesets of their own each unique to the individual monster or enemy. Unlike Monster
Hunter or Elden Ring, however, swimming should be included as a feature. The combat must feature
combos, do not make this cheap with just basic moves.

As the player progresses they will earn gil and level up, allowing them to upgrade their
weapons, armor, materia, pick up new jobs, and eventually build a party to join them on their
missions.

The story should be very dynamic and engaging, but should never push the player's character into
too legendary of a status, they should always just be an adventurer. The story should, however,
have plenty of cameos with the Scions of the Seventh Dawn and the legendary Warrior of Light.
Always keeping them looking cool and powerful. Include many dynamic cutscenes."

Follow-up message from the user during the asset-pipeline step (verbatim):
"No skin culling"

Interpretation applied: no body skin is culled, hidden or deleted underneath clothing. Every base
keeps its full, intact body mesh (the unclothed base bodies from the "A" files are used), and
clothing pieces are layered on top of it. No triangles of the body are removed at build time or
hidden at runtime.

2. PROVIDED ASSETS
---------------------------------------------------------
HyurBases.zip:     HyurFA.vrm, HyurMA.vrm (base bodies), HyurFSampleClothes.vrm, HyurMSampleClothes.vrm
MiqoteBases.zip:   MiqoFA.vrm, MiqoMA.vrm (base bodies with cat ears and fox-style tail),
                   MiqoFSampleClothes.vrm, MiqoMSampleClothes.vrm
HairstyleSamples.zip: HairstyleSample.vrm, HairstyleSample2.vrm (hair on a female Hyur body)

Findings from inspecting the VRM (VRM 1.0, VRoid) files:
- Each file has a Face mesh (7 material primitives, 57 blend shapes) and a merged Body mesh whose
  primitives are split by material: body skin, smallclothes ("Onepiece"), scalp hair layer
  ("HairBack"), and in the sample files Tops, Bottoms, Shoes and a Baseball Cap; Miqo'te files
  also contain CatEar and FoxTail. Hairstyle files add a separate Hair mesh.
- All primitives of a merged mesh share one vertex buffer, so parts must be compacted.
- Skeletons use the standard J_Bip humanoid bones plus secondary chains (J_Sec, J_Opt) for
  spring bones. Rest pose is a T-pose, model faces +Z.
- Materials are MToon (toon) with KHR_materials_unlit.

3. TECHNOLOGY DECISIONS
---------------------------------------------------------
- Engine: Godot 4.3 stable at first; upgraded to Godot 4.5.1 in round 2 (see section 7).
  Chosen because it can export a Windows .exe from this Linux environment, imports glTF natively,
  and has a fast native animation system. Export templates downloaded from GitHub releases.
- Language: GDScript, everything built from code and data (very few scene files) so content is
  data-driven and easy to extend.
- Output: Build/EorzeaHunter.exe (84 MB engine) + Build/EorzeaHunter.pck (game data, ~30 MB).
  The pack is kept separate so no single file exceeds GitHub's 100 MB limit.
- Asset pipeline (tools/vrm_split.py, Python + numpy + Pillow):
  * Base bodies: face + full body skin + smallclothes. Nothing under clothing is culled.
  * Hairstyles: only the hair primitives (hair + its scalp layer) are kept; the sample body is
    discarded. Hair is attached to whichever model uses it.
  * Clothing: each item (tops, bottoms, shoes, cap) is isolated into its own file; the sample
    body is discarded.
  * Miqo'te ears and tail are separated so races can include or omit them.
  * All parts are rebound to the standard humanoid bones (secondary-bone weights folded into the
    nearest humanoid ancestor) so any part fits any base skeleton.
  * Face blend shapes trimmed from 57 to the 8 used (neutral, angry, joy, sorrow, surprised,
    blink, mouth A, mouth O).
  * MToon replaced by physically based materials (roughness per surface type: skin, hair, eyes,
    cloth); placeholder normal/emission maps removed; hair and iris textures stored as neutral
    luminance so the creator's colour pickers drive the hue.
  * Textures de-duplicated by content hash (31 unique), capped at 1024 px, imported as
    GPU-compressed S3TC/BPTC with mipmaps.
- Races not provided as bases (Elezen, Lalafell, Roegadyn, Au Ra, Hrothgar, Viera) are built from
  the Hyur/Miqo'te bases with bone-scale profiles (height, torso width with counter-scaled
  shoulders, head size), plus procedural pointed ears (Elezen/Lalafell), horns and a scaled tail
  (Au Ra), stretched ears (Viera) and cat ears/tail (Miqo'te, Hrothgar).
- Animations: VRoid models have none, so a procedural animation library (anim_lib.gd) authors
  ~55 animations from limb poses (idle, walk, run, sprint, strafe, jump, fall, roll, backstep,
  guard, hit, knockdown, death, lying, sitting up, kneeling, talking, waving, swimming, diving,
  gathering, drinking, dashing, leaping, and ~30 attack/cast/shoot/dance/iaijutsu animations).
  They are generated once per skeleton and played by AnimationPlayer.
- Zone connections verified with web searches against FFXIV wiki pages (Mor Dhona borders
  Coerthas Central Highlands and Northern Thanalan; Coerthas Central Highlands borders Foundation,
  North Shroud and Mor Dhona; Eastern Thanalan borders South Shroud; La Noscea zone borders;
  Western Thanalan's Vesper Bay ferry to Limsa Lominsa).
- Job starting cities follow where each job's guild/quest begins in FFXIV:
  Gridania: WHM, DRG, BRD, GNB.  Limsa Lominsa: WAR, SCH, SMN, NIN, DNC, SGE.
  Ul'dah: PLD, MNK, BLM, SAM, RDM.  Ishgard (Foundation): DRK, AST, MCH, RPR.
  Viper and Pictomancer were left out because they arrive in Dawntrail, after this story's setting.

7. ROUND 2: BLACK SCREEN AND DOWNLOAD FORMAT
---------------------------------------------------------
User message (verbatim):
"The game launches but remains a black screen, also downloading the build folder is an annoying
way to download the game"

Investigation:
- Installed Wine and ran the actual Windows .exe against the sandbox's software Vulkan driver.
  The Godot 4.3 build reached the title screen within 20 seconds, so the black screen is
  specific to real Windows graphics drivers and could not be reproduced exactly.
- Most likely cause: Godot 4.3 falls back from Vulkan to Direct3D 12 when Vulkan has problems,
  and that fallback can render black without an extra shader DLL; 4.3 has no OpenGL fallback.

Changes:
- Upgraded the engine to Godot 4.5.1, which falls back Vulkan -> Direct3D 12 -> OpenGL
  automatically. (Godot 4.6.1 was tried first, but its Windows binary crashed at startup under
  this Wine version, so it could not be verified and was not used.)
- Added an OpenGL (Compatibility renderer) path: ground telegraphs draw as flat quads because
  decals are unavailable there, water depth math adjusted for OpenGL, and unsupported effects
  (SSAO, SSIL, SSR, volumetric fog, subsurface scattering, TAA/FXAA, FSR) are turned off
  automatically. Removed a project-level anti-aliasing default that errored on OpenGL.
- Added a boot splash image ("Loading...") so first launch never shows a plain black window
  while shaders compile.
- Fixed the pause menu selecting a tab before its tabs were built.
- Distribution: the game data is now embedded in the exe, shipped as a single
  EorzeaHunter-Windows.zip (43 MB) in the repository root, containing EorzeaHunter.exe,
  "Play in Safe Mode (OpenGL).bat" (forces the OpenGL renderer) and "READ ME.txt". The old
  Build/ folder was removed.

Verification:
- Windows exe under Wine: both Vulkan and forced OpenGL reach the title screen with no game errors.
- Linux build on 4.5.1: all 19 jobs, all 20 missions and all 12 cutscenes pass the automated
  suites; a rendered playthrough on the OpenGL renderer (city, field, combat, swimming, menu)
  works.

8. ROUND 3: UI EXTENDING OFF-SCREEN
---------------------------------------------------------
User message (verbatim):
"UI elements are extending offscreen. Ensure all UI elements are locked to the bounds of the screen"

Causes found:
- Panels were placed with fixed pixel offsets and grew with their content; nothing stopped them
  growing past the screen edge. The title-screen settings list was taller than the screen, and
  long rows (save-slot summaries, smithy/market entries) could widen panels.
- In windowed mode the 1600x900 window could be larger than a small or DPI-scaled monitor.

Changes:
- New UI.FitBox container: every screen's panels are laid out at their natural size inside the
  screen margins and scaled down uniformly whenever they would not fit, so they can never cross
  the screen edge. Applied to the HUD (bars and hotbar, party list, zone/objective tracker,
  compass, boss bar, prompt, toasts, banner, gil line), dialogue window, cutscene titles and
  skip hint, town service windows, pause menu, title screen (menu, slot lists, settings,
  disclaimer) and character creator (editor panel and bottom buttons).
- The title-screen settings list now scrolls inside its panel.
- Long button labels shorten with "..." (full text in the tooltip) instead of widening panels;
  save-slot summaries clip; the quest objective text wraps.
- Windowed mode now sizes and centres the window inside the monitor's usable area (taskbar
  excluded), keeping 16:9.
- Fixed creator and pause-menu tabs being renamed to internal names (e.g. "@ScrollContainer@300")
  after a rebuild, because old tabs were still present when new ones were added.

Verification:
- New automated check opens 49 screens (title menus, every creator tab for both characters, HUD
  with long toasts/banner/objective, every pause-menu tab, every town service, dialogue) and
  measures every visible UI element's on-screen rectangle. Zero elements cross the screen edge at
  1600x900, 1280x720, 1024x768 (4:3) and 2560x1080 (ultrawide). The same check on the previous
  layout flagged the overflowing settings list, confirming it detects the problem.
- Windows exe boots to the title screen under Wine with no errors; zip rebuilt (43 MB).

9. ROUND 4: FACES, CAMERA, MARKERS, INVENTORY, LIGHTING
---------------------------------------------------------
User message (verbatim, with a screenshot of the washed-out character creator):
"Eyeline should inherit the skin tone.
Character inventory is not interactable
Attack markers clip into terrain rather than being projected onto it
Talking to characters often zooms the camera in but does not show the character due to height
and terrain differences, ensure the conversation camera is looking at their actual face.
Include a feature to hide weapons when sheathed.
Make use of VRoid's expressions and visemes to make the characters' mouths move when they are
talking and make their expressions match the scenes
As can be seen in the provided screenshot, the character creation screen is extremely washed
out, give it more well rounded lighting"

Changes:
- Eyeline and mouth layers of the VRoid face are now tinted with the chosen skin tone, matching
  the face skin.
- Inventory (pause menu > Items) is interactive: use consumables, choose the quick item used
  with the potion button, discard materials, meld materia into the weapon or remove it, and
  show/hide individual armour pieces (hidden pieces still protect).
- Attack markers are now meshes draped over the terrain (and water surface) with the fill
  animated in a shader, replacing decals and flat quads. They follow slopes on both the Vulkan
  and OpenGL renderers and never sink into the ground.
- Conversation cameras aim at the speaker's real head position (head bone), adjusting for race
  size, height and uneven ground, and check line of sight: if a wall, pillar or the player's
  body blocks the face, the camera swings around the speaker to the nearest clear angle. Town
  NPC chats now also use a face-framing conversation camera.
- New option: Settings > "Hide weapons while sheathed".
- Faces: the asset pipeline now keeps VRoid's expressions (angry, fun, joy, sorrow, surprised),
  blink and all five vowel visemes (A, I, U, E, O). Mouths follow the dialogue text as it
  types (vowels map to visemes); every story line has an expression matching the scene;
  expressions blend smoothly and blinking pauses during smiles. Town NPCs smile when chatting.
- Character creator lighting: three-point studio lighting (warm key with soft shadows, cool fill,
  rim light), dim backdrop, modest ambient, matte plinth, AgX tone mapping and SSAO, so skin keeps
  its shading instead of washing out. The lights no longer draw a sun disc in the backdrop.
- Found and fixed during testing: town NPCs kept wandering during conversations (they now stop
  and face the player), story scenes with the guildmaster spawned a second copy away from the
  real one (scenes are now staged at the real guildmaster, whose standing copy is hidden), shots
  were framed for human-sized speakers (distance now scales with the speaker's size), NPC name
  tags loomed over close-up shots (hidden while talking), and service NPCs (guildmaster, cook,
  smith, merchant, trainer...) faced into their buildings (they now face the plaza).

Verification:
- Rendered tests (OpenGL renderer in the sandbox): creator lighting, joy/angry expressions with
  A/O visemes, guildmaster and citizen conversations framed on the speaker's face, interactive
  inventory, hidden sheathed weapons, and markers draped over a hillside and lake surface.
- Headless suites: 19/19 jobs, 20/20 missions, 12/12 cutscenes pass.
- Windows exe under Wine boots to the title screen on Vulkan and OpenGL with no errors.

11. ROUND 6: CHARACTER CREATOR REVAMP (FROM A REFERENCE VIDEO)
================================================================================
User request: "Revamp the character creator to closely match what is shown in videos", with a
Google Drive link to a ~6 minute 1080p60 screen recording of the FFXIV (Dawntrail) character
creator. The video was downloaded, and still frames were extracted with ffmpeg (one every 2.5 s
plus full-resolution key frames) to study the layout. No video or audio ships with the game.

12. ROUND 7: GRIDANIA AS ONE CITY WITH REAL TUNNELS
================================================================================
User request: a Google Drive video tour of Gridania (16 minutes, 1080p60), "recreate it as
accurately as possible. Disregard UI elements. There are three paths between New Gridania and
Old Gridania that are represented by lines of glowing blue orbs which when touched move the player
to the other instance, but for this game I want those tunnels to be real tunnels that directly
lead to the other part of the city. The two sides of the city should not be separate instances,
and the orb lines should not be there."

Approach:
- The video was downloaded, sampled every 3 seconds with ffmpeg, studied for the city's feel and
  landmarks, and then deleted. Gridania was rebuilt as an original stylised layout in the game's
  own procedural style: it keeps the real district and landmark names and the New/Old structure,
  but the level geometry is not traced from the footage (that layout is Square Enix's work).
- New file scripts/world/gridania_builder.gd (GridaniaBuilder). CityBuilder hands Gridania to it;
  the old Gridania-only branches in CityBuilder were removed.

Layout:
- A forested rock ridge (moss on top, trees on the crest, boulders and rock buttresses on both
  faces) runs east to west and divides the city. Three tunnels (west, centre, east) pass straight
  through it: 6.5 m wide, timber frames every 4 m, plank ceilings, lanterns and a warm light in
  each. Signs at each mouth point to the other half. Walking through takes you from one district
  to the other in the same zone; the area title ("New Gridania" / "Old Gridania") appears when you
  cross (World.district_at and info["districts"]).
- New Gridania (south): Aetheryte Plaza on a raised timber deck ringed by golden canopies; the
  Carline Canopy (Adventurers' Guild: guildmaster, levemete, party recruiter) and the Roost
  (inn); Carpenters' Guild (The Knot, smithing); the Adders' Nest; the Archers' Guild (Quiver's
  Hold); Acorn Orchard; a river with three timber bridges; Apkallu Falls at its west end; the
  Blue Badger Gate to the Central Shroud.
- Old Gridania (north): Stillglade Fane (Conjurers' Guild, an open round pavilion before giant
  trees); the Lancers' Guild (job trainer); the Leatherworkers' Guild (market); the Botanists'
  Guild; Mih Khetto's Amphitheatre; the Lotus Stand (a swimmable pond with lily pads); the White
  Wolf Gate to the North Shroud (moved to the Old Gridania side).
- The zone's display name is now "Gridania" (the aetheryte is still "New Gridania").
- Terrain fix: zones can set their own shoreline height (biome "shore_h"); before, the whole flat
  city floor was coloured as sand.

Tests: all three tunnels walked end to end with real movement; every service NPC present and
standing in the open; arrivals from both neighbouring zones land on clear ground; story talk
steps, all missions and all 33 cutscenes still pass.

12. ROUND 7: GRIDANIA REBUILT AS ONE CONTINUOUS CITY
================================================================================
User request: recreate Gridania from a provided 16-minute video tour (shared by Google Drive), make
the three New/Old Gridania orb crossings real walk-through tunnels with no instance split, keep it
lore-accurate, and raise visual quality well above the earlier "cheap" look.

Agreement reached during the round: the city is not a copy of the game's map or of specific
buildings. The video is used as a reference for design language and the kinds of decoration found
in the city (and for the rough arrangement of landmarks); streets, terrain and buildings are this
game's own design.

First pass (checkpoint) feedback from the user: the city read as a square, the ground was still
flat, landmarks were poorly arranged, the aetheryte was not visible, there was no airship landing
on a cliff, houses looked like generic medieval timber-framing, buildings had no interiors, and
flowers, fences, aethernet shards and a marketplace were missing; the video had not been used as a
style reference. All of these were addressed in the second pass:
- Terrain: an irregular, lobed, cliff-ringed basin (1.25 m heightmap) with passes for the Blue
  Badger and White Wolf gates; a wooded ridge between New Gridania (lower, south) and Old Gridania
  (higher, north) cut by three stone-vaulted tunnels with portals, wing walls and lanterns;
  raised terraces (Aetheryte Plaza, the Lancers' Guild, the great tree above the Lotus Stand);
  an eastern cliff shelf 18 m up with a gorge beyond it; winding swimmable streams, pools,
  Apkallu Falls and a northern spring fall; mossy rock outcrops along cliffs and the ridge.
- Placement: Aetheryte Plaza at the heart of New Gridania; the Carline Canopy at the foot of the
  eastern cliff with switchback stairs up to the Airship Landing (deck over the gorge, docked
  airship, ticketer); the Adders' Nest nearby; the marketplace by the stream; the Knot across the
  water; the Archers' Guild and Acorn Orchard to the west; Old Gridania's square with the
  Leatherworkers' Guild on its boardwalk, the Lancers' Guild terrace, Mih Khetto's Amphitheatre,
  the Lotus Stand over its pool, Stillglade Fane on its island and the Botanists' Guild gardens.
- Architecture (GStyle): dark plank-walled lodges on stone plinths with raised front decks and
  stairs, arched double doors with iron straps, tall arched windows, bell-curved roofs in
  terracotta, red or moss green, round turrets and cottages with domed roofs, gold leaf-shaped
  awnings and flower barrels at the doors.
- Interiors: the guild hall, the Knot, the Leatherworkers', Lancers', Archers', Botanists' guilds
  and the Adders' Nest are walk-in: open doorways, counters, shelves of goods, tables and stools,
  rugs, stained glass, banners, a gallery reached by stairs, hanging lamps and warm lights.
  Service NPCs (guildmaster, levemete, recruiter, innkeep, smith, merchant, trainer) stand inside.
- The Aetheryte: a great crystal within curved brass arms on a stepped dais. Aethernet shards at
  eleven points work as in-city fast travel.
- Dressing: a marketplace of stalls heaped with goods and vendors, flower beds, flowering shrubs,
  planters, fences along banks and gardens, bridges with ring medallions in the railings, lantern
  posts, and the generated forest (buttress-rooted giants, oaks, saplings, bushes, ferns).
- Tech: shaders/surface.gdshader (procedural stone, timber, plaster, shingle, bark, rock, planks,
  moss), leafcard.gdshader (leaves, fern fronds, blossoms), waterfall and light-shaft shaders,
  cobbled streets in the terrain shader, "stream" water kind for swimming, NPC floor heights for
  interiors, and rasterised lookup grids so the city builds in under a second.
